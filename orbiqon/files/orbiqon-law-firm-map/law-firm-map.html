The automation map for law firms

Everything we'd automate in a small or mid-size US firm, department by department: what each piece does, the software it lives in, and the person who approves it. Use it to plan your own setup, or to check what an agency is offering you.

Most firms don't have a single big problem. They have small leaks in every department: an enquiry nobody sorts, a letter retyped from the intake form, a client who calls to ask where their case is, an hour of work that never makes it onto a timesheet.

03Where we'd start: billing

Time capture is the first build we'd suggest for most firms, for three reasons:

  • It finds money you already earned. Work that happened but never reached a timesheet.
  • No client ever sees it. If a draft entry is wrong, the only person who notices is you, before anything is billed.
  • It's small. It needs read access to email and calendar, your matter list in Clio, and your billing codes.
How the approval works: every Friday you get one list of draft entries. Approve, edit or delete each one. Nothing goes on a bill until you say so.

04Where custom software beats off-the-shelf

Sometimes the gap isn't a missing automation, it's a missing tool. Common ones we see for firms:

  • A client status page built from your Clio milestones, for clients who will never log in to a portal.
  • An internal dashboard showing intake, unbilled time and overdue invoices in one place for the managing partner.
  • Your own product. If your firm has a process other firms would pay for, that can become software. We build MVPs too.

05The rules every build follows

No system gives legal advice, quotes a fee, or assesses a case. It collects facts, drafts, sorts and reminds. A person at the firm decides.
  • Client data stays in your systems (your practice software and tools under a signed agreement), never pasted into a public chatbot.
  • Every draft has a named approver, listed in the map above.
  • Lawyers stay responsible for AI-assisted work. ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) covers competence, confidentiality, client communication and fees when firms use generative AI. Check your state bar's guidance too.

065 questions to ask any agency before you buy

  1. Does it work inside my practice software, or beside it?Ask them to show it writing into Clio or Lawmatics live, not a demo account.
  2. Where does client data go?Get a list of every service that touches it, and the agreement for each.
  3. Who approves each step?If the answer is "nobody, it's fully automatic", walk away.
  4. Who fixes it when it breaks?A name and a response time, in writing.
  5. What's the smallest first build?A good partner proves one piece before selling you the rest.
